Investing.com - Jefferies reiterated a Buy rating and $115.00 price target on Lululemon Athletica (NASDAQ:LULU), noting improvements in product assortment while cautioning that recovery remains a multiquarter process. The stock currently trades at $113.82, and [InvestingPro](https://www.investing.com/pro/LULU) data suggests the company appears undervalued based on its Fair Value analysis.

The firm’s recent store visits showed the retailer’s assortment moving away from rust, burnt orange, and chocolate-heavy colors highlighted in May toward cleaner presentations with more neutral, brand-appropriate colors. Merchandising appeared more organized with less cluttered floors and fewer disjointed product categories.

Consumer metrics remained under pressure in recent months. Purchase consideration weakened 5.2% in June following declines of 3.7% in May and 3.8% in April. Foot traffic in May fell approximately 4% year-over-year and roughly 8% on a two-year basis. First-quarter fiscal 2026 Americas comparable sales declined 5%, or 6% in constant currency.

Profitability continued to compress as gross margin declined 410 basis points in the first quarter. Management guided to an additional 50 basis points of markdown pressure in the second quarter. Selling, general and administrative expenses deleveraged 310 basis points, driving operating margin down approximately 730 basis points to 11.2%. An [InvestingPro](https://www.investing.com/pro/LULU) tip notes that 21 analysts have revised their earnings downwards for the upcoming period, reflecting ongoing headwinds. Incoming CEO Heidi O’Neill begins in September, making any strategic repositioning more likely a 2027 development.

In other recent news, Lululemon Athletica has faced a series of analyst updates and price target adjustments. Morgan Stanley resumed coverage of the company with an underweight rating, expressing concerns about the growth potential in China and the need for a structural adjustment in the Americas region. Freedom Broker upgraded its rating to hold from sell, although it lowered the price target to $139, reflecting management’s latest outlook. Bernstein SocGen Group reduced its price target to $145, citing unsuccessful product launches and negative social media feedback, while maintaining a Market Perform rating.

Truist Securities also lowered its price target to $115, pointing to ongoing brand and product challenges and murky turnaround prospects.
